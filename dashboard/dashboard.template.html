    Grants × Patents

    A US patent from federally funded work carries a government-interest statement, such as “This invention was made with government support under AI066487 awarded by the NIH.” A grant is linked to a patent when that statement names it. A number with its activity code (R43 AI066487) names that award only; a bare number (AI066487) names every stage of the NIH project, so R21/R33 and R43/R44 pairs are both linked.

    Sources. Grants: NIH RePORTER projects and publications (federal-biomedical-tech-datasets-script). Papers: the papers workstream's bundle, and PubMed records of grant-cited and patent-cited papers, with their grant lists and reference lists. Patents: USPTO Patent Public Search and Google Patents, including each patent's government-interest statement and citation tables (patent_pipeline, filovirus fetch patent-details).
    Grant years are the project start and end dates. 106 grants have none in RePORTER, so their first and last fiscal year in the pull is used (marked “fiscal years” in the Grants table).
    Explore the graph uses the knowledge graph itself (Postgres core.* exported to Neo4j): taxa, isolates, sequences, proteins, gene families, structures, epitopes and assays, antibodies, countermeasures, clinical studies, and the same grants, papers and patents. A step is one relationship in either direction. Every node is walked through, however many links it has, since some highly connected ones (each gene family, which every protein of every filovirus points to) are the bridges between viruses. But past the starting record, each record passes on only its best links, if you choose a limit under “Links followed per record” (all links by default), ranked by edge confidence (curated links, which have none, count as certain, above uncertain keyword matches) and then by how connected the neighbor is. That is SerenQA's neighbor scoring (Appendix D.3), which limits what it shows the LLM the same way; without it, two steps from almost anything runs through a hub and reaches most of the graph. The graph itself keeps every link.
    Viruses and proteins come from each workstream's own keyword matching against the knowledge graph vocabulary (kg_keywords.csv), mapped onto one list here: Ebola viruses, Marburg viruses, the filovirus family named generally, and the nine filovirus proteins. Patent matches are candidates; some are flagged for review.
